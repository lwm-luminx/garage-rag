Garage for macOS · app icon

Six directions for the Garage icon

Garage indexes your files on your Mac, uploads nothing, and hands an assistant only the excerpt it asked for. The icon should say one of those things in a single shape that still reads at 16 pixels in the menu bar.

One shape, centredThe mark sits inside Apple's rounded-square mask (1024 canvas, 824 body, ~22% corners). No scene, no floor, no reflections outside the mask.
Flat with one lighting cueA gentle top-to-bottom gradient and at most one shadow. macOS 26 renders icons as layered glass through Icon Composer, so each mockup is drawn as two or three flat layers that can be exported separately.
Survives 16 pxEvery direction is shown at Dock and menu-bar sizes, with a monochrome template glyph that the menu-bar item can share with the SF Symbol style.
Own the huePurple is already Garage's colour on the site and in the current icon. Four directions keep it, two try a material instead, because a warm object in a Dock full of blue and purple tiles is easier to find.

Today

The current icon is a rendered scene: a purple glass tile on the floor of a garage, surrounded by books. The tile and the mark inside it are good raw material. The scene around it is what fights macOS: at 32 px the books turn into grey noise, the tile floats off-centre inside the mask, and the glyph, which is about a fifth of the canvas, drops below the size where it can be read. The mark itself is also hard to name. Three of the six directions below keep the purple; the last one keeps the mark.

1. The roll-up door

A closed garage door with light showing under the bottom panel. Your things are inside, the door is down, and something is switched on in there.

This is the one direction that says the product's name and its privacy story in the same shape. Four horizontal panels are a pattern that survives any size, and the strip of purple light under the door carries the brand colour without needing a purple tile. The steel-blue field keeps it distinct from Finder and Mail in the Dock.

Icon Composer layers: field, door frame, door panels, light strip. The light strip is the one that gets the glass specular.

2. The excerpt

A page stays on the tile. One line of it is lifted out as a small dark card. That card is what an assistant gets; the page is what it never sees.

This draws the sentence on the site's landing page, "your assistant gets only the excerpts it searches for, never the whole collection", as a picture. It keeps the purple tile so the change from today's icon is an evolution rather than a rebrand. The dark snippet card is the element to remember, and it reads at 16 px as a page with a bar across it.

Risk: a document icon is a crowded category (Pages, Preview, TextEdit). The dark card and the purple field do the separating.

3. The catalog drawer

A library card-catalog drawer: wood, a brass label holder, a brass pull. Retrieval as an object you have opened before.

Garage is a retrieval system before it is anything else, and the card catalog is the one retrieval object everyone recognises. Apple still ships material icons (Notes, Books, Contacts), so wood and brass are within convention. The label can carry a range like "Ga–Gz" or a single letter G. This is the most distinctive tile in a Dock, and the only one of the six with no purple at all.

Risk: it says "library", not "private", and the brass pull needs care at 32 px.

4. The house of vectors

A house outline on a dark field, with a small embedding graph inside it. The index lives at home.

This is the icon for people who know what pgvector is. The house is the privacy claim, the graph is the technology, and a dark tile with a light outline is the current fashion for developer tools (Warp, Ghostty, Zed). It is honest about what Garage is, and it will look right next to Terminal and Xcode.

Risk: the graph collapses to dots at 16 px, so the template glyph drops to three nodes. It is also the least warm of the six, and the site's tone is warm.

5. The G lens

A single heavy G whose bowl is a magnifier and whose tail is the handle. Search, and the initial, in one stroke.

The safest macOS shape: a white glyph on a saturated tile, like Messages, FaceTime or Music. It is unmistakable at every size and it is the easiest to turn into an SF Symbol-style menu-bar glyph, a favicon and a document icon. The whole mark is two stroked paths, so it costs nothing to iterate on weight and the gap of the G.

Risk: it says nothing about privacy or the garage, and a magnifier-plus-letter is a well-worn device.

6. The current mark, cleaned up

Keep today's glyph, drop the garage scene, fill the mask with the purple tile, and scale the mark up to about 45% of the canvas.

The least work and the least risk. It fixes every convention problem with the current icon (scene, off-centre tile, tiny glyph) and keeps the identity people have already seen on the site and in TestFlight. The mark is redrawn here as clean geometry, a cross with two leaves and two folded page corners, so it can be built as layers.

Risk: the mark is still hard to name. Combined with direction 1 or 2 later, it would become the site logo rather than the app icon.

Where I would start

  1. The roll-up door (1) if the icon should carry the name and the promise. It is the only direction that a stranger could describe back to you.
  2. The excerpt (2) if continuity with the purple matters more. It says the one thing the site works hardest to say.
  3. The cleaned-up mark (6) as the fallback for 1.5 if there is no time for a new identity before the store submission. It is a few hours in Icon Composer, and it removes every reason a reviewer would flag the icon.

Every mockup is plain SVG in this page's source, so any of them can be pulled out and imported into Icon Composer as a starting point. Nothing in the repo has changed.
